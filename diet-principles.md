# Principles for Incremental Dietary Improvement

- **Prefer whole foods**: Choose whole or minimally processed foods (frozen vegetables and tinned legumes count) over highly processed snacks, ready meals and fast food.
- **Eat more vegetables**: Add vegetables to meals you already eat, aiming to fill half your plate.
- **Eat more fruit**: Have whole fruit as a snack or with meals, working towards two serves a day.
- **Choose wholegrains**: Swap white bread, rice, pasta and cereals for wholegrain or wholemeal versions to boost your fibre intake.
- **Eat more legumes and nuts**: Have a handful of unsalted nuts or seeds most days, and add beans, lentils or chickpeas to meals several times a week.
- **Include dairy or alternatives**: Have milk, yoghurt, cheese or calcium-fortified alternatives most days, favouring unflavoured varieties.
- **Vary your plant foods**: Rotate different vegetables, fruits, wholegrains, legumes and nuts through the week rather than relying on the same few.
- **Swap to healthier fats**: Use olive, canola or other plant oils instead of butter, ghee or coconut oil.
- **Choose healthier proteins**: Rotate legumes, fish (ideally twice a week), eggs, poultry and red meat, keeping red meat to a few meals a week and processed meats like bacon and ham occasional.
- **Make water your default drink**: Choose water, unsweetened tea or coffee, or plain milk over soft drinks, cordial, energy drinks and juice.
- **Sweeten with fruit and spice**: Add fresh, frozen or a little dried fruit, cinnamon or vanilla to unsweetened cereal, yoghurt and porridge instead of sugar, honey or syrup.
- **Reduce salt**: Choose lower-salt versions of everyday foods like bread, stock and sauces, and flavour with herbs, spices, garlic or citrus instead.

## Overall Philosophy

Treat these principles as preferences, not hard rules. Aim for gradual, sustainable improvement through small additions and swaps rather than sweeping changes: add nutritious foods so they crowd out less nutritious ones, and cut back through swaps rather than bans, keeping room for foods you enjoy. Start with the foods you eat most often, and repeat each change until it becomes habit. Consistency matters more than perfection, and your overall pattern matters more than any single meal. The goal is to progressively improve your default diet, not to follow a restrictive one.
