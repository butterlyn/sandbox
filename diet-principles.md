# Principles for Incremental Dietary Improvement

- **Prefer whole foods**: Choose whole or minimally processed foods over highly processed alternatives.
- **Prioritise fibre**: Favour wholegrains, legumes and other high-fibre foods over refined alternatives.
- **Eat more vegetables**: Add vegetables to existing meals and aim for greater variety.
- **Eat more fruit**: Choose whole fruit as a regular snack or addition to meals.
- **Choose healthier fats**: Prefer unsaturated fats from olive oil, nuts, seeds, avocado and fish.
- **Improve protein quality**: Favour fish, legumes, eggs, lean meats and other minimally processed protein sources.
- **Eat more legumes, nuts and seeds**: Incorporate these nutrient-dense foods into meals and snacks.
- **Drink more water**: Make water your default drink over sugary beverages.
- **Reduce added sugar**: Prefer foods with less added sugar, particularly everyday staples.
- **Moderate salt intake**: Choose lower-sodium alternatives where convenient.
- **Increase dietary variety**: Eat a wider range of plant foods and other nutritious ingredients.
- **Add rather than restrict**: Focus on introducing nutritious foods rather than eliminating enjoyable ones.

## Overall Philosophy

Treat these principles as preferences, not hard rules. Aim for gradual, sustainable improvement through small substitutions and additions rather than sweeping dietary changes. Make healthier choices more frequently, especially in foods you eat regularly, without striving for perfection or eliminating foods you enjoy. The goal is to progressively improve your default diet, not to follow a restrictive one.
